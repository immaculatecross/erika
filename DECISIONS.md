## D-1 · 2026-07-17 · Name and concept: Erika — "it studies you"

The operator settled the name Erika. The concept: a correction layer over the user's real speech, for advanced speakers whose remaining errors are fossilized and personal. Erika derives the curriculum from the speaker's own recorded life, not from invented course content.

## D-2 · 2026-07-17 · Local-first web v1; no auth

Next.js (App Router, TypeScript strict) + Tailwind + better-sqlite3, running on the user's machine. No accounts in v1; OAuth and hosting are E-7. **Why:** zero infrastructure, privacy by construction (day-long recordings capture bystanders), and API routes that a later mobile client can reuse.

## D-3 · 2026-07-17 · Native audio analysis, never speech-to-text

Analysis calls `gpt-audio-1.5` (fallback `gpt-audio`) with audio input, chunked into ~10-minute mono MP3 segments to stay under request limits. **Why:** a transcript erases exactly the signal an advanced learner needs — pronunciation, hesitation, the almost-right word. Verified live at founding: a planted-error recording came back with every mistake identified, corrected, and explained.

## D-4 · 2026-07-17 · DESIGN.md is binding; no component library

Strict monochrome design constitution, hand-rolled components, system font stack. **Why:** many fresh worker sessions must produce one coherent, Apple-grade product; a written constitution is the only shared taste they can have. A library would fight the aesthetic and bloat the bundle.

## D-5 · 2026-07-17 · v0.1 spans E-1…E-4 (foundation → flashcards)

Four missions, overriding ideate's one-or-two-mission default. **Why:** the operator explicitly wants to exercise long-horizon dispatch ("test your ability in long horizon tasks"). The same-day-mergeable rule still applies to each individual mission.

## D-6 · 2026-07-17 · Private repo under immaculatecross; PRs + CI from mission one

github.com/immaculatecross/erika, private (author identity per mfactory D-08). Every mission is a real PR with CI checks (lint, typecheck, test, build, tripwires). Branch protection on private repos needs a paid plan — if GitHub refuses it, arming it is the operator's preflight item and the dispatcher merges only on an approving review verdict meanwhile.

## D-7 · 2026-07-17 · System ffmpeg/ffprobe is a prerequisite

Transcoding and duration probing shell out to the system ffmpeg (present on the dev machine, v7.1). No bundled binary. **Why:** a ~70 MB static dependency versus a one-line brew install; revisit at E-14 when the app leaves this machine.

## D-8 · 2026-07-17 · Design is Apple-grade, not monochrome (amends D-4's constitution content)

The operator clarified: "monochrome" meant elegance, not the absence of color. DESIGN.md is rewritten around the Apple system palette (neutral canvas, one indigo accent, muted semantic colors), translucency and depth, and spring-based motion. ## D-9 · 2026-07-17 · Day-scale capture: dumps up to 24 hours (amends D-3's 30-minute framing)

Capture accepts files up to 24 h / 2 GB, streamed to disk and processed as resumable async ingest jobs — never inside a blocking request. **Why:** the real source is an always-on recorder carried through a whole day; thirty minutes was caution, not design.

## D-10 · 2026-07-17 · Cost is an architecture problem: extract speech, then cascade

Nothing reaches a model until voice-activity detection has stripped silence and noise (a day dump typically collapses to 1–2 h of actual speech). Then a cascade: `gpt-audio-mini` triages time-compressed (1.25–1.5×) segments and flags suspicious windows; `gpt-audio-1.5` deep-listens only those windows at native speed, where pronunciation fidelity matters. Segments are content-hashed and cached — nothing is ever billed twice. Every run shows a cost estimate from an editable rates table before starting, and a monthly budget cap in Settings halts autonomous runs truthfully. **Why:** naive day-long analysis on the deep model would cost tens of dollars per day; VAD plus the cascade cuts billed model-time by an order of magnitude or more.

## D-18 · 2026-07-23 · Correction-forward, error-once (amends E-5 card fronts and E-9 presentation)

The user's errors are **never practice stimuli**: card fronts and exercise prompts are meaning-first (English gloss or Italian context gap) and the retrieval target is always the correct form. The original utterance appears exactly **once**, at feedback time — correction headlined, the error subordinate and unmistakably marked — then retires. Contrastive playback and the slips dossier stand (deliberate noticing and analytics, not drills). **Why:** SLA's noticing hypothesis and the hypercorrection effect argue for one clear confrontation; lure-familiarity research argues against ever rehearsing the wrong form. The operator's instinct ("mistakes sink in") settled the drilling half; the evidence settled the noticing half. **Consequences:** E-28 retrofits cards, phrasebook, and the report; an optional strict-hide toggle is deferred until someone asks.

## D-19 · 2026-07-23 · The knowledge model: append-only production evidence, FSRS-6 strength

An append-only `evidence` log (sources: findings, exercises, tutor turns, placement) with mode weights — spontaneous 1.0 ≫ cued 0.6 ≫ recognition 0.3, ×0.7 discount when audio-derived — from which per-item knowledge state (lemma+POS with lazy sense splits, grammar rule, phone) is **derived and rebuildable**, never stored as source truth. Scheduling: **FSRS-6 via `ts-fsrs` (MIT)** replaces SM-2 everywhere; retrievability R(t,S) is the one strength scalar for every item kind; existing cards are state-seeded (S≈interval, ease→difficulty), reviews logged as evidence, parameters optimized later. "Known" requires corroboration: ≥2 correct events on ≥2 days, ≥1 spontaneous, never audio-only, none incorrect since. **Production evidence only** — no ambient-exposure mining of other speakers (operator call, 2026-07-23; upholds D-2's privacy stance). Production evidence includes what the user gets **right**: the deep pass also emits correctly-produced lemma+POS (morph-it-validated), written as discounted spontaneous-correct evidence — Record teaches the model the user's real vocabulary, not only their errors, and recording-attested lemmas are excluded from new-item selection. Lexicon: ~15k lemmas from license-clean sources (FrequencyWords CC BY-SA through a morph-it lookup table in SQLite); CC BY-NC resources (Kelly, itWaC, spaCy models) stay reference-only, out of the shipped data path. Grammar: an LLM-authored ~180–250-rule prerequisite DAG structured after the *Profilo della lingua italiana*, operator-checked in review. **Why:** evidence types differ in strength, audio evidence is noisy, and a derived state survives model improvements; FSRS tolerates the irregular timing production evidence has and SM-2 does not.

## D-20 · 2026-07-23 · Spend for signal: the richness dial (amends D-10's posture, keeps its architecture)

The operator's call: spend more for the richest picture of the user's behavior. Captures **≤30 min skip triage** — 100% deep-listen at native speed with an enriched prompt (pronunciation suspects, colto register upgrades, disfluencies) ≈ $0.22 per 10-min capture. Day dumps keep VAD + cascade with triage loosened toward ~50% flagged ≈ $1.77 per 12-h dump (Batch API on the deep leg optional, −50%). D-3 stands: never STT for error detection. `rates.ts` is recalibrated — the deep model truly costs ~$0.02/audio-min audio-in, **~$0.03/audio-min all-in with text output, roughly half the currently ledgered $0.06** (the all-in figure is the recalibration target) — and the default cap rises to match the posture. Enriched observations persist (new finding categories or a structured notes channel — the work order decides) and deep max-tokens rises so truncation repairs stay rare. Ledger, estimates, caching, and lease-before-spend are unchanged.

## D-21 · 2026-07-23 · Pronunciation: the LLM flags, Azure scores, clean audio only (extends D-3)

Phone-level judgments from audio LLMs are unreliable — they diagnose from L1 stereotypes over acoustics (evidence in `docs/research/spike-3`). So the deep pass only **flags suspects** (gemination, vowel aperture, stress), and scoring happens on scripted re-record drills via **Azure Pronunciation Assessment** (it-IT, phoneme-level scores, ~$1/audio-hour). Scripted assessment of a known drill text is not D-3's banned STT — nothing free-spoken is transcribed for error detection. Ambient day-dump audio is never pronunciation-scored. **Consequences:** a second provider secret (`AZURE_SPEECH_*`) enters `.env.local` at E-37; Azure spend enters the same `spend_ledger` under the same cap.

**Amended 2026-07-24 at E-37 — RE-VALIDATED and CONFIRMED, with the balance of the split restated.** Live research before the build (`observations/OBS-002-e37-pronunciation-landscape.md`, all sources retrieved 2026-07-24) checked D-21 against current vendor documentation and found nothing that contradicts it. Both of its own figures check out: the PA locale table still lists `it-IT`, and PA bills at baseline speech-to-text rates. Verified findings:

- **OpenAI still cannot *measure* pronunciation.** No phoneme output, no reference-text scoring, no pronunciation-assessment endpoint anywhere in the API surface (checked against the OpenAPI-generated SDK on `main`, the authoritative machine-readable statement). It can *describe* how something sounded; it cannot score it. `gpt-realtime-2.1` remains current, so E-34's model choice is unaffected.
- **Microsoft moved toward this split.** Content assessment (preview) is **retired** from Speech SDK ≥ 1.46.0, and the docs now direct developers to an LLM for the qualitative layer — the vendor publishing D-21's own division of labour.
- **Clarification for it-IT: PA returns no prosody score and no syllable grouping** (both `en-US` only), and therefore none of the prosody-derived error types. So **no intonation or rhythm number may appear in the Italian UI** — that feedback stays on the LLM-flag side. `EnableProsodyAssessment` is never set: for Italian it yields nothing and it is the only add-on-billed score, so asking for it would buy a bigger invoice and no data.

**And the operator's steer, taken 2026-07-24, which sets the priorities inside the split:** **finding the mistakes is the critical capability, and it lives with the LLM** — the `pronunciation` finding category plus the `notes.pronunciation` richness channel are the input that matters. The critical *experience* is the loop **hear it pronounced correctly → say it back**, built on the E-33 rendition Erika already caches under the same cap; it must be fully valuable with **no Azure and no score**. **Azure PA is therefore an optional scorer for specialised drills — explicitly nice-to-have, never a precondition for the pronunciation experience, and never called from the Record/analysis path.** Absent a key the studio is complete, not degraded.

**A standing honesty limit, not a temporary one.** Score→feedback thresholds are **our own tunable choices, transferred from English-language norms**. There is **no labelled Italian pronunciation-assessment corpus in existence**, open or commercial — every benchmark in the field (speechocean762, L2-ARCTIC, TIMIT, Buckeye, EpaDB) is English. So the mapping cannot be validated against Italian ground truth by anyone, and a live `AZURE_SPEECH_KEY` buys **real fixtures, not validation**; validating would take building an Italian learner corpus that does not exist. What the UI may claim is bounded accordingly: these are Azure's model outputs, the bands are ours, and Erika never presents an LLM's impression as a score (D-19).

## D-22 · 2026-07-23 · Speaker filtering is local and recall-first (fulfils E-13's founding promise)

Speaker verification runs **on-device** (sherpa-onnx embeddings; ~45 s enrollment take; centroid match, max over 3–5 s windows). Recall-first: drop only confident-non-user; uncertain flows through to analysis; the threshold is calibrated against a committed two-real-voice labelled fixture (D-13 pattern, user recall ≥0.99). Excluded audio is flagged and counted visibly, never deleted. Fallback: session-level clustering of the same embeddings; still unsure fails open to analyze-everything. A kill-switch env disables the filter. **Hosted speaker-ID is never the default** — bystander audio never leaves the device.

## D-23 · 2026-07-23 · Register: italiano colto, as a dial

A Settings dial — colloquiale → standard → colto → letterario — **default colto**, injected into analysis recasts, lesson generation, the tutor persona, and TTS instructions. Reading/listening draws on the public-domain canon (Manzoni, Dante, Pirandello, Verga). The product is usable by natives polishing their register. "Dante-level" is the ambition, not the grammar: elevated contemporary Italian, no archaisms.

## D-24 · 2026-07-23 · The habit layer is calm (scopes E-12's "no gamification")

Learn gets exactly: a **streak** counting any completed daily goal, with two automatic silent repairs per month ("Day 14 · repaired Tue"); one **ink goal ring**; one **factual completion moment** per day; a **knowledge map** whose cells tint toward green only through resolved-slip semantics — green stays mastery, never activity. Banned: confetti, mascots, XP/points/levels, leaderboards, badges, purchasable anything, loss-guilt copy, more than one celebratory beat per day. DESIGN.md's Copy rule is amended in the same PR: factual acknowledgment is not cheerleading. The editor's letter stays outside the mechanics. Streak days are **local days**, stated explicitly (the E-22 UTC lesson).

## D-25 · 2026-07-23 · The API boundary is codified; uploads are tus (extends D-2)

The audit found the boundary already clean — every page fetches JSON from `/api/*`; no server component touches SQLite; no filesystem paths leave the server. Codified for all new work: every capability ships as a JSON `/api` route first; one error envelope `{ error: { code, message } }`; a no-op auth middleware stamps the single-user principal today; additive changes only, a version prefix only when native ships. ## D-26 · 2026-07-25 · v0.7 is the simplification: one session a day, no buttons, one voice (redefines v0.7; supersedes E-39's scope)

The operator drove v0.6 — the version FEATURES.md calls "feature-complete" — and returned a verdict the test suite could not: *"this is not really a great polished usable consumer product… it seems more like a work in progress thing."* The named symptoms are all **concept count**, not breakage: costs displayed live with many customizable moving parts; an **Analyze** button you must press before anything is analyzed; several different kinds of lesson; flashcards whose prompt face is the literal word *grammar* or *phrasing*; no onboarding on an empty database; and a realtime tutor that *"listens very well, but does not speak super well."* The standard set is explicit: *"a good consumer product like Duolingo would have just one session per day, very clear, no questions asked, super polished, super straightforward."* And, restated as the general principle: *"we did lots of things maybe overcomplicating a lot of stuff. We wanna have a sleek consumer product."*

**So v0.7 stops being E-39's debt sweep and becomes the simplification: E-42…E-46.** Subtraction is the deliverable. Every milestone must remove more concept than it adds and must name, in its PR, what it deleted — a milestone that only adds a nicer screen on top of the old ones has failed this decision. The owed E-39 defects are **not** dropped: each is absorbed by the milestone that rewrites its surface (operator's ruling, *"simplify, absorbing defects"*), so no effort is spent hardening code about to be replaced. E-39 keeps only the leftovers and moves to v0.8.

**What is not simplified: the extraction.** PRODUCT.md's promise stands unchanged — *"the differentiator is learning and harvesting as much data as we can about the user through recordings."* D-3 (native audio, never STT for error detection), D-10/D-20 (the cascade and the richness dial), and D-19 (the knowledge model) are untouched. What gets simpler is the **interface and the number of concepts the learner must hold**, never the depth of what Erika hears.

**The rulings:**

- **The tutor leaves the Realtime API.** It becomes a turn-based **STT → LLM → TTS** loop behind a two-interface vendor seam (OpenAI first; Cartesia or another vendor swappable without touching the loop). Realtime's listening was never the problem; its Italian voice was. A **minimum conversation duration**, settable and shown, is what validates the day. This amends E-34's *transport*, not its intent — persona, `log_evidence`, the one spend spine and the recording→analysis path all survive.
- **Capture has no buttons.** Record → one confirmation → upload, ingest **and analysis** all automatic. The analysis report stays — it is good — but becomes a place you may open, never a step you must pass.
- **Money leaves the flow.** One budget and one spend figure, in Settings. Fourteen cost surfaces become one. The app must still never lie about what it costs (RETRO-004's never-waivable class is unchanged) — honesty **moves**, it does not disappear, and automatic analysis makes disclosing the standing cost *more* obligatory, not less.
- **One session a day, linear:** a lesson of five minutes or less (a grammar/conjugation rule, or about ten new words, or a small mix) → drills answered by **click or voice only, never typing** → a spoken conversation → done. The day's goal is that session. Today the goal counts flashcards alone (`lessonsDone` is hard-coded `0`), so a lesson and a whole conversation contribute nothing to the streak — that is the mechanical root of the "pile of optional errands" feeling.
- **No card front may be a bare category word.** Cards are *inspired by* what the recordings taught and must be answerable by someone who does not remember the mistake they came from — which is everyone. D-18 is unaffected: the error is still never the stimulus.
- **Onboarding is mandatory on an empty database** and adds one or two spoken prompts, so level is inferred from real production as well as recognition.
- **Progress becomes a real surface** — *"show progress but make it sexy and user friendly."* This ratifies the operator-gated RETRO-002 P6 proposal and retires the dev-only inspector.
- **Demote, never delete.** Focus, phrasebook, archive, slips, readings, shadow, studio and the letter all stay reachable behind one Library entry. Where a demoted surface earns a place in the daily flow it may take one — the editor's letter as a once-a-week beat inside the session is the operator's own example.

**Why now.** v0.6 closed as *"the best-tested version of this project and the least usable"* (STATE.md). Feature completeness arrived without usability, so the remaining gap is not features and cannot be closed by more of them. **Consequences:** FEATURES.md gains E-42…E-46 and v0.7 is redefined; D-17's two-tab shell, D-18, D-19, D-22, D-23 and D-24's calm-habit rules and ban list all stand unchanged; D-21's studio survives as a Library surface and inside the daily drill step; `gpt-realtime-*` leaves the product's runtime path.

**Amended 2026-07-25 on approval — product authority.** The operator approved the v0.7 plan with a standing directive: *"aim for a really complete, usable, intuitive consumer product… each one of those can have solutions — really make product calls, after thinking well and justifying them a little bit."* So the bar for every v0.7 milestone is not its acceptance criteria but a sentence: **a person who has never seen this repository can use the thing end to end, without asking a question, and want to come back tomorrow.** A ticked criterion with that sentence still false is an unfinished milestone. Workers hold product authority inside their milestone's scope — interaction, copy, the affordance the brief failed to name, the ambiguity it left — and may contradict an acceptance criterion provided they say so and argue it; the price is a written justification per call in the PR body, and the standing prohibitions are unchanged (the binding decisions above, another milestone's scope, and D-26's own rule that subtraction wins ties).

## D-27 · 2026-07-25 · The daily lesson's backbone is the syllabus; recordings are the overlay (amends D-17 and D-19's composer ordering)

The operator, clarifying what "one session a day, like Duolingo" actually means: *"you should really mostly integrate the vocabulary and grammar and stuff that is in the knowledge base that we worked on, and only integrate the findings from the recordings if there are any. But the backbone should be your lessons, like Duolingo."*

This **inverts** D-17's stated priority. D-17 promised a course *"composed from the user's own recorded mistakes first, canonical material only when that well runs dry"*, and D-19 built the lexicon and syllabus as the fallback. From now on the relationship is the other way round: **the 30,786-lemma frequency lexicon and the 266-rule A1→C2 syllabus (E-26) are the spine of the daily lesson, and findings from the learner's recordings are an overlay applied when there are any.**

**Why this is right, and why it does not weaken the product.** A curriculum built only from recordings is only as regular as the recordings, and a learner who has not recorded in ten days would open the app to nothing — the failure mode that makes a daily habit impossible. Duolingo is effortless partly because it always has a next thing, and it always has a next thing because it owns its content. Erika now owns its content too: E-26 shipped a real syllabus on a proven-acyclic prerequisite DAG and a band-labelled lexicon, and until now that asset has only ever been a fallback.

**The differentiator is not what this trades away.** "It studies you" (D-1) continues to live where it always did and is, if anything, sharpened by having a reliable base to modulate: the analysis of real speech, the placement that starts you at your own edge, the tutor primed with your active slips, the cards minted from your own mistakes, the progress surface, and *which* syllabus rules and lexicon bands you are shown at all. What changes is only that the **lesson always exists**, and your recordings make it *yours* rather than making it *possible*.

**Consequences.** `lib/compose.ts`'s ordering changes: FSRS-due reviews stay first (spaced repetition is not negotiable), then the lesson is drawn from the syllabus/lexicon at the learner's knowledge edge, with slips and unspent findings **woven in when present** rather than being the source the lesson is built from. A day with no recordings, no findings and no slips must still produce a complete, non-degraded session — that is now an acceptance criterion, not a fallback path. WO-E44 and WO-E45 are amended.

## D-28 · 2026-07-25 · The tutor listens as natively as the analysis does — never through a transcript (extends D-3 to the tutor; corrects D-26's transport ruling)

The operator: *"for listening in recording, and trying to understand mistakes, I think realtime might be good."*

They are right, and D-26 had a real flaw. It replaced the whole tutor with STT → LLM → TTS, which would have made the tutor detect the learner's mistakes **from a transcript** — precisely what D-3 forbids, and for precisely the reason D-3 gives: *"a transcript erases exactly the signal an advanced learner needs — pronunciation, hesitation, the almost-right word."* A tutor whose entire purpose is catching mistakes an advanced speaker still makes cannot be the one surface in this product that listens through text. The founding verification of D-3 was a planted-error recording that a native-audio model caught in full; a Whisper transcript would have shown clean sentences.

**So the two legs are decided separately, because only one of them was ever broken.**

- **Listening stays native audio.** The tutor hears the learner with an audio-native model, exactly as the analysis path does. STT may be used for a **scripted, known-answer drill response** (D-21's existing allowance — nothing free-spoken is transcribed for error detection), and nowhere else.
- **Speaking becomes TTS.** This was always the only defect: *"it listens very well, but it does not speak super well."* `alloy` and `nova`, plain synthesis, per the operator's ear.

**The transport is chosen by measurement, not by preference.** Two candidates: **(A)** the Realtime API with audio in and **text out**, keeping its turn detection and its listening quality — which the operator has praised from real use — and synthesizing the reply through TTS; **(B)** a turn-based loop whose listening leg is the `gpt-audio` family this repo already trusts for error detection, dropping WebRTC entirely. Judge both on whether the tutor still catches pronunciation and hesitation errors, and on per-turn latency. **Default to (A) where they are close**, on the strength of the operator's direct experience. D-26's cost and simplification arguments still apply to everything downstream of the listening leg.

**Not optional under either choice:** the stale-lease overbill is fixed or deleted (`RESERVATION_STALE_MS` 15 min < `maxTutorSessionMinutes()` 30 min, so a legal 21-minute call bills 1.9×), the TTS rate is repriced from per-character to per-audio-output-token, and the minimum-duration rule stands. If (A) wins, the WebRTC and ephemeral-secret machinery survives on merit — but the money defects do not survive with it.

**D-28 settled 2026-07-25 by measurement — transport (A), and D-26's cost argument is inverted.** `docs/research/spike-6-tutor-listening.md` ran ~130 live calls ($0.74) and answered the gating question first: a Realtime session **does** accept `output_modalities: ["text"]`, confirmed both over WebSocket (`session.updated`) and through this product's own mint route. So audio in, text out, TTS voice is buildable. Three results decided it, and two of them contradict what D-26 assumed:

- **Accuracy.** Both transports caught 5 of 7 planted errors with **zero hallucinations** — but every `gpt-audio` model **missed `"ho andato"` in 9 of 9 attempts**, with the correction written verbatim in its own prompt from `lib/mistakes.ts`. Both Realtime models caught it every time. For a tutor whose entire job is catching an advanced speaker's residual errors, that is not a tie.
- **Cost, inverted.** Taking the reply as *text* removes the $64/1M audio-output leg, which makes **(A) cheaper than (B)** — $0.283 vs $0.340 per 10-minute conversation — and (A) achieved a 96%+ prompt-cache hit rate where (B) got **zero** cached tokens across ~50 calls. D-26 argued the migration away from Realtime on cost; on the listening leg that argument does not hold.
- **Simplification, smaller than claimed.** (B) removes ~510 lines and 7 concepts but **adds 4**, and browser-side turn detection is harder than anything it deletes — with a failure mode that *manufactures false corrections* by cutting a learner off mid-sentence. D-26's expectation that E-43 would delete ~1,000 lines was wrong, and E-43's "What this deletes" section shrinks accordingly. **Deleting less is the correct outcome here; do not delete to hit a number.**

**And D-3 was measured rather than merely asserted.** `whisper-1` silently *corrected* the spike's planted errors — `familia` → `famiglia`, `note` → `notte` — turning a learner's mistake into clean text before any model could see it. That is D-3's founding claim, demonstrated on this project's own fixtures, and it is the concrete reason D-28 exists.

**The honest cost of compliance:** native listening bills ~2.7× a pure transcript loop ($0.283 vs spike-5's measured $0.10–0.11 per 10 minutes). We are paying that deliberately, to hear what a transcript erases. It remains far below the current all-audio Realtime path.

**Rate defects the spike found, all in the unsafe direction, now owed:** `gpt-audio-mini` at $0.006/audio-min measures **$0.0067 at analysis scale** (under-priced on the Record path *today* → E-42); `gpt-audio-1.5` at $0.03 measures **$0.0574 on a 5 s turn** — 1.9× under — because `callCost` bills per audio-minute and charges nothing for a prompt re-sent every call, so **a per-minute rate is not a safe floor for short calls** (→ E-42/E-43); and `REALTIME_RATES` carries **no text-token rates at all**, so `realtimePerMinuteUsd` over-books 5.1× — the safe direction, but large enough to refuse a learner who has budget (→ E-43). Separately the spike found a **live Record-path bug**: models return the category `"vocabulary and word choice"`, which `normalizeCategory` rejects, **discarding the whole segment's findings** (→ E-42, in repair).

**Not yet verified:** the **WebRTC** leg end to end — the spike proved the session contract over WebSocket, which needs a browser to close. E-43 owns that.

**Amended 2026-07-25 (second time) — cost visibility returns, and the tier becomes the learner's choice.** Shown the honest price of flagship Realtime audio-out — a 10-minute conversation books ~$1.25 against a measured ~$0.66, so a daily conversation would consume ~78% of the default $50 cap — the operator ruled: *"we can't spend one hundred dollars per month per user… either we use realtime mini, and I'm not sure if this works or not — worth putting that in the settings that I can try… the most urgent thing is to have something that works really well, and then I just wanna see how much it costs in practice. So maybe you leave that in the app for now, just show how much that has cost."*

Three consequences, and the second is a **partial reversal of D-26** made deliberately:

1. **The model tier returns as a Settings choice** — flagship (default) or `gpt-realtime-2.1-mini` — so the operator can judge mini's usability themselves rather than have spike-6's 9-turn sample decide it. Both tiers' rates must clear their floors; pricing only the default would make the cheaper option the unsafe one.
2. **Actual cost becomes visible in the app again.** D-26 removed *pre-run estimates* from the flow and that stands — nothing says "est. $X" before you act. But **what a conversation actually cost, after it happened**, and month-to-date spend in Settings, are now required. The distinction is the point: an estimate before an action is a toll booth and a moving part; a factual record of what was spent is the app telling the truth about itself, which is what D-26 demanded in the first place. Report the committed actual, never the reservation, never a projection.
3. **Deep cost optimisation is deferred to its own mission, explicitly.** The operator: *"that might be a whole other mission, and I don't think it's most urgent right now."* Only optimisations that cannot cost quality land now — shorter replies (which also cuts latency) and verifying that prompt caching still works under audio-out. Trimming conversation history, shortening the persona against its guardrails, or restructuring the session to save tokens are **recorded as recommendations, not implemented**. The default cap stays **$50**; raising it would answer the wrong question.

## D-29 · 2026-07-26 · Lesson teaching content is Italian and prepared before launch (amends D-17/D-18)

D-17's sentence *"Italian-only until excellent; the app speaks English"* now has an explicit boundary: global chrome, navigation and system notices may remain English, but **every learner-visible field inside a lesson is target-language-only Italian** — title, explanation, examples, vocabulary definitions, new-word examples, exercise cues, choices, answers and rationales. D-18's permission for an English-gloss practice front is withdrawn for lessons: meaning-first now uses an Italian definition or a correct Italian context gap, still never the learner's erroneous form.

The daily lesson is also a one-lesson-ahead asset. As soon as the composer selects today's item, the Learn home prepares its complete lesson under the existing claim, shared budget and spend ledger. Start is unavailable only while a real billable preparation is in flight.
